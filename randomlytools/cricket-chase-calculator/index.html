Cricket Chase Calculator: How It Works

A cricket chase calculator shows how many runs a team still needs to reach a target and how quickly it must score. It can be useful during a live T20 or ODI chase and for hypothetical target scenarios.

Runs Required = Target − Current Score

Required Run Rate = Runs Required ÷ Overs Remaining

Cricket Overs and Balls

Cricket overs use ball notation rather than decimal notation. For example, 10.3 means 10 completed overs and 3 legal balls: 63 balls or 10.5 mathematical overs. Likewise, 19.3 means 117 legal balls.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many runs are required to chase?

Subtract the current score from the target score. If the target is 200 and the current score is 90, 110 runs are required.

What is required run rate in a chase?

It is the average number of runs per over needed from the remaining overs to reach the target.

What does 10.3 overs mean?

It means 10 overs and 3 legal balls, equal to 63 balls or 10.5 mathematical overs.

Can a chase calculator predict the winner?

No. A mathematical chase calculator shows required rates and projections; it does not guarantee a match result.
