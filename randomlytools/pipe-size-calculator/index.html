How Pipe Sizing Is Calculated

Pipe sizing by velocity starts with the relationship between volumetric flow rate, pipe cross-sectional area and average flow velocity.

Q = A × V

For a circular pipe, the cross-sectional area is A = πD² / 4. Combining these relationships gives a diameter calculation from flow rate and target velocity. The calculator converts your selected units before applying the formula.

Pipe Size Calculator Inputs

The calculator has three modes. Pipe Size estimates the internal diameter needed for a selected flow rate and target velocity. Flow Velocity calculates average velocity from flow rate and internal diameter. Flow Rate calculates volumetric flow from internal diameter and velocity.

Supported flow-rate units include GPM, litres per minute, cubic metres per hour, cubic metres per second and cubic feet per minute. Diameter can be entered in inches, millimetres, centimetres or feet, and velocity can be entered in feet per second or metres per second.

Pipe Sizing and Nominal Pipe Size

The calculated diameter is an internal diameter, not a nominal pipe-size designation. Nominal pipe size is a standardized label and the actual internal diameter varies with pipe standard, material and wall thickness. For that reason, a final nominal size should be selected from the applicable pipe-size table after the required internal diameter has been established.

Why Pipe Diameter Matters

For a fixed flow rate, reducing the internal diameter increases the average velocity because the same volume of fluid passes through a smaller cross-sectional area. Increasing the diameter has the opposite effect. In real piping design, velocity is only one part of the selection process; pressure loss and other project conditions can also matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate pipe size from flow rate?

Use Q = A × V and A = πD²/4. After converting flow rate and velocity to compatible units, solve for the required internal diameter.

How do you calculate flow velocity in a pipe?

Divide volumetric flow rate by the pipe's cross-sectional area. For a circular pipe, the relationship is v = 4Q/(πD²) with compatible units.

Does pipe diameter affect flow velocity?

Yes. At a fixed flow rate, a smaller internal diameter produces a higher average velocity and a larger internal diameter produces a lower average velocity.

What is the difference between pipe diameter and nominal pipe size?

Internal diameter is the actual inside opening used for flow calculations. Nominal pipe size is a standardized designation and is not necessarily equal to the measured internal diameter.

Can this calculator select a final engineering pipe size?

It provides a flow-and-velocity diameter estimate. Final selection can require pressure-loss calculations, pipe material, temperature, fittings, applicable codes and project-specific engineering review.
