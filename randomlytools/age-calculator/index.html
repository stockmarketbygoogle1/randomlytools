Age Calculator: Calculate Your Exact Age from Date of Birth

An age calculator by date of birth helps you find your chronological age from a complete birth date. Enter your date of birth and choose the date on which you want to calculate your age. The calculator shows your completed age in years, months and days, along with the total number of elapsed days, your next birthday and the weekday on which you were born.

Unlike a simple subtraction of birth years, an exact age calculation has to consider whether the birthday has already occurred, the different number of days in each month, and leap years. This is why a date-of-birth calculator is useful when you need an exact calendar-age result rather than an approximate number of years.

How to Use This Age Calculator

  1. Enter your date of birth: Select your complete birth date, including day, month and year.
  2. Choose the calculation date: The default is today, but you can select another valid date to calculate your age on a specific day.
  3. Click Calculate Age: The tool compares both calendar dates and calculates the completed years, remaining months and days.
  4. Review the results: You can see your exact age, total days, next birthday, days until your birthday and day of birth.

Using the full date of birth gives a more precise result than entering only a birth year. If your birthday has not occurred yet in the selected year, the calculator correctly keeps the completed age one year lower than a simple year-to-year subtraction.

What Is an Age Calculator?

An age calculator is an online utility that calculates the time elapsed between a person's date of birth and a selected reference date. Most everyday age calculations use the chronological-age convention: your age increases by one on each birthday.

For example, someone born on January 15, 2000 is 26 years old on January 15, 2026. On January 14, 2026, the same person is still 25 years old because the 26th birthday has not occurred yet.

How to Calculate Age from Date of Birth

To calculate age from date of birth, compare the birth date with the date on which you want to know the person's age.

  1. Subtract the birth year from the calculation year.
  2. Check whether the birthday has already happened in the calculation year.
  3. If the birthday has not happened, subtract one from the completed-year result.
  4. For an exact result, calculate the remaining calendar months and days after the last completed birthday.

For example, if the date of birth is June 10, 2000 and the calculation date is September 20, 2026, the completed age is 26 years. From June 10 to September 20 is another 3 months and 10 days, so the exact calendar age is 26 years, 3 months and 10 days.

Age Calculator Formula

For completed years, the basic formula is:

Completed age = calculation year − birth year

If the birthday has not occurred yet in the calculation year:

Completed age = calculation year − birth year − 1

The years-months-days result is more detailed. After determining the completed years, the calculator finds the remaining calendar months and days. When the day of the calculation date is earlier than the birth-day number, it borrows days from the previous calendar month. When necessary, a month is also borrowed while calculating the completed years.

This calendar method is preferable to dividing total days by 365 when the result needs to be expressed as years, months and days. A year may contain 365 or 366 days, while months have different lengths.

Why You Cannot Always Calculate Age by Simply Subtracting Years

A common shortcut is to subtract the birth year from the current year. That gives the right completed age only when the birthday has already occurred. If today's date is before the birthday, the result is one year too high.

For example, a person born on December 20, 2000 is not yet 26 on September 20, 2026. Their completed age is 25 because their 26th birthday is still in the future. The exact calendar age is 25 years and 9 months on that date.

Age in Years, Months and Days

Many people searching for an exact age calculator want more than the number of completed years. This calculator therefore reports the remaining months and days as part of the calendar-age result.

Suppose a person was born on March 10, 2010 and the selected date is September 20, 2026. Their result is 16 years, 6 months and 10 days. The months and days are based on actual calendar dates, not an assumption that every month contains the same number of days.

Examples include checking age on an exam date, application deadline, joining date, appointment date, historical event, anniversary or another important date. The result is based on the selected reference date rather than the current day.

How Many Days Old Am I?

The Total Days result shows the elapsed number of calendar days between the date of birth and the selected calculation date. This is different from multiplying your age by 365 because leap years can add an extra day.

Total days are useful when you want a day-level measure of the time between two dates. For very long periods, the number naturally includes leap days that occurred between the two dates.

How Many Days Until My Next Birthday?

The calculator finds the next occurrence of your birth month and day and shows the number of days remaining until that birthday. If the selected calculation date is already your birthday, the result indicates that your birthday is today.

The next-birthday result is based on the selected calculation date, so you can also use the calculator to see the birthday immediately following a future or historical reference date.

Leap Years and February 29 Birthdays

Leap years matter when calculating exact age and total elapsed days. A leap year has 366 days instead of 365, with February containing 29 days.

If someone was born on February 29, their birth date occurs on February 29 only in leap years. For a non-leap year, different legal and cultural conventions may be used when celebrating or defining the birthday. This calculator focuses on calendar-date arithmetic and should not be treated as a legal-age determination for a particular jurisdiction.

For official applications, exams, immigration, employment, voting, driving or other regulated purposes, always check the age rule stated by the relevant authority because a form may define the qualifying date differently.

Month-End Dates and Exact Age

Months do not all have the same number of days. This can make dates near the end of a month less intuitive. For example, a period beginning on January 31 does not have the same simple day structure as a period beginning on January 15.

An exact age calculator therefore works with calendar years, months and days rather than treating every month as a fixed number of days. This is important when you need a human-readable result such as years, months and days.

An age calculation is often needed while filling out forms, applications and eligibility information. The important point is that an organization's age requirement may be based on a specific cut-off date, not necessarily the date on which you fill out the form.

How to Calculate Age in Excel

You can also calculate age from a date of birth in Microsoft Excel. A commonly used approach is the DATEDIF function. If the date of birth is in cell A2 and you want completed years as of today, a typical formula is:

=DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"Y")

For remaining months and days, additional DATEDIF calculations can be used:

  • =DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"YM") for completed months after the last birthday.
  • =DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"MD") for remaining days after completed months.

Excel formulas can be useful for spreadsheets containing many dates, while an online age calculator is quicker when you only need an individual result.

What Is the Difference Between Age and Date Difference?

Age normally describes a person's completed calendar years plus the remaining months and days since birth. A date-difference calculation simply measures the interval between two dates.

For example, two dates can be separated by a certain number of days, but expressing that interval as a person's age requires the birth date and the birthday-based calendar convention. That is why an age calculator and a generic date-difference calculator may present results differently.

Common Age Calculation Mistakes

  • Using only the birth year: This can make the completed age one year too high before the birthday.
  • Ignoring leap years: Long date ranges can contain February 29 and therefore cannot always be treated as 365-day years.
  • Assuming every month has 30 days: Calendar months have 28, 29, 30 or 31 days.
  • Using today's age for a cut-off-date requirement: Official applications may use a specific reference date.
  • Confusing total days with calendar age: Dividing days by 365 does not directly produce an exact years-months-days age.
