Guides & Articles

Useful, practical guides for using RandomlyTools calculators and random generators.

How These Guides Can Help

These guides explain how to use RandomlyTools and how to understand the results from its calculators and generators. They include practical examples, formulas, selection tips, and common questions so you can choose the right tool instead of guessing which one to use.

For example, the attendance guide explains the percentage formula and class planning, while the number and team guides cover practical ways to make random selections for classrooms, sports, games, meetings, and events.

Browse by Topic

Students: attendance, classroom activities, and number-selection guides. Numbers: random number generation, no-repeat selection, and number picker comparisons. Names & usernames: gaming, Instagram, business, shop, restaurant, and other naming ideas. Groups & teams: random assignments for sports, classes, workshops, and events. Everyday tools: internet speed and other practical calculator guidance.

How to Use a Guide

  1. Choose the topic that matches what you are trying to do.
  2. Read the examples and rules before entering values into a related tool.
  3. Open the linked RandomlyTools calculator or generator when you are ready to try it.
  4. For calculations, check the inputs and understand that the result depends on the values you provide.

These pages are educational and practical references. They are intended to help you use the tools correctly, not to guarantee a particular outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these guides free to read?

Yes. The guides are available to read online, and the related RandomlyTools calculators and generators are designed for free use.

Do the guides explain the formulas used by calculators?

Where a calculator uses a standard formula, the relevant guide explains the calculation or points you to the related tool page for more details.

Can I use these guides for school or group activities?

Yes. Several guides cover classroom participation, random names, numbers, groups, and sports teams. Choose the guide that matches your activity and follow its practical steps.

Where can I find the actual calculator or generator?

Each relevant article links to a RandomlyTools tool so you can move from the explanation to the interactive calculator or generator.
