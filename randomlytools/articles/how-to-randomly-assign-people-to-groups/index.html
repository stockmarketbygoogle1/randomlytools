How to Randomly Assign People to Groups

Random grouping is a quick way to create teams without spending several minutes deciding who belongs together. It works for classrooms, workshops, office activities, parties, sports practice, and games.

Two simple methods

Choose a group count

Decide how many groups you need, enter every participant once, then distribute names randomly across the groups. When possible, keep group sizes as even as the total number allows.

Choose a group size

If you know you want groups of four, shuffle the participant list and place names into groups of four until everyone is assigned.

How to create fair random groups

  1. Make one clean participant list.
  2. Remove duplicates and people who are not eligible.
  3. Decide the number or size of groups.
  4. Randomize before manually changing assignments.
  5. Check only for practical issues such as an absent participant.

When random grouping is useful

  • Classrooms: discussion, projects and activities.
  • Workplaces: workshops, brainstorming and team-building.
  • Sports: practice squads and informal games.
  • Parties: trivia, challenges and game teams.
  • Events: breakout sessions and networking groups.

Should groups be skill-balanced?

Pure random grouping is not the same as skill-balanced grouping. If equal skill is important, you may need a separate balancing step. Do that only after the random assignment and explain the rule clearly.

FAQs

How do I randomly split 20 people into 4 groups?

Use a list of all 20 people and distribute them randomly into four groups of five.

Should everyone have one entry?

Yes, when each participant should have an equal chance of landing in any group.

Can random groups have different sizes?

They can, but equal or near-equal sizes are usually easier to manage.

Can I prevent two people from being together?

You can, but that becomes a constrained assignment rather than purely random grouping.
