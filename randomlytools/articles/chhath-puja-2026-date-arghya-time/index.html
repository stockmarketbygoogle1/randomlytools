Chhath Puja 2026: Date, 4-Day Calendar and City-Wise Arghya Time

Chhath Puja in 2026 will be observed from Friday, 13 November to Monday, 16 November. The main evening offering, Sandhya Arghya, falls on Sunday, 15 November 2026, and Usha Arghya is offered at sunrise on Monday, 16 November 2026.

This guide brings together the four-day calendar and approximate sunset and sunrise timings for major Indian cities. Since Arghya is offered according to the local Sun position, the exact time changes from city to city.

What Are the Four Days of Chhath Puja?

1. Nahay Khay – 13 November 2026

Chhath begins with Nahay Khay. Devotees take a ritual bath, prepare for the observance and eat a simple, traditionally prepared meal. The day marks the beginning of the disciplined four-day festival.

2. Kharna – 14 November 2026

On Kharna, devotees observe a fast during the day. After the evening worship, the traditional prasad is prepared and offered, commonly including kheer and roti. The strict fast that follows continues through the main offerings.

3. Sandhya Arghya – 15 November 2026

On the third day, devotees gather near rivers, ponds and other water bodies to offer Arghya to the setting Sun. This is the main evening offering of Chhath Puja. The local sunset time is therefore important for planning.

4. Usha Arghya – 16 November 2026

On the final morning, devotees offer Arghya to the rising Sun. After the morning rituals and prayers, the fast is completed through Parana according to family tradition and local observance.

Sandhya Arghya vs Usha Arghya

Sandhya Arghya is the offering made to the setting Sun on the evening of 15 November 2026. Usha Arghya is the offering made to the rising Sun on the morning of 16 November 2026. Both are central parts of the festival, but they take place on different days and use the local sunset and sunrise as their timing reference.

Why Is Chhath Puja Celebrated?

Chhath is a traditional Hindu festival dedicated to the worship of Surya, the Sun, and Chhathi Maiya. Devotees express gratitude for the Sun's life-sustaining energy and pray for the well-being, health and prosperity of their families. The observance is especially prominent in Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern Uttar Pradesh and among communities living elsewhere in India and abroad.
