SSC Exams Kya Hote Hain?

The Staff Selection Commission conducts recruitment examinations for different posts and qualification levels. The exam name alone is not enough to decide whether you are eligible: the notification for that examination tells you the educational qualification, age requirement, post-specific conditions and other rules.

For a quick starting point, candidates commonly compare exams such as CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD, CPO, JE, Stenographer and Selection Post. The right exam depends on your qualification and the requirements of the particular recruitment.

Kaunsa SSC Exam Aapke Qualification Ke Liye Hai?

A simple way to narrow the list is to start with the qualification you already have. That does not give you the final eligibility decision, but it tells you which examination notices are worth reading first.

If you are 10th pass

Start by checking 10th-level recruitments such as SSC MTS and SSC GD, along with relevant Selection Post notices. The exact qualification, age and other conditions belong to the individual notification.

If you are 12th pass

SSC CHSL and SSC Stenographer are common exams to check. Some posts can have additional subject or skill requirements, so “12th pass” should not be treated as the complete eligibility test.

If you are a graduate

SSC CGL and SSC CPO are among the graduate-level examinations candidates commonly consider. CGL also has post-specific requirements, so having a degree does not automatically mean that every CGL post has identical eligibility.

What if graduation is still ongoing?

This is one of the situations where a simple yes/no answer can be misleading. You need to check the educational-qualification cut-off date stated in the relevant examination notification. For example, the SSC CGL 2026 notice specifies the applicable qualification conditions in the examination notice, so candidates should read that notice rather than rely on a general “final-year students are eligible” statement. citeturn0search49

SSC Age Limit Kaise Check Kare?

Do not check your age by looking only at the number printed beside an exam name. SSC usually states a specific date on which age is reckoned, and different posts can have different age bands.

The practical method is:

  1. Choose the exact SSC examination and, where relevant, the post.
  2. Find the age-reckoning or crucial date in the latest notification.
  3. Read the permitted age range or DOB range.
  4. Compare your date of birth with that range.
  5. Check whether an applicable age relaxation category changes the upper limit.

For example, the SSC GD 2026 notice fixed 01-01-2026 as the crucial date and specified an 18–23 age requirement, corresponding to the stated DOB range, with notified relaxations for eligible categories. citeturn0search51

The SSC CGL 2026 notice is a good example of why one universal age number is unsafe: it lists different age limits for different groups of posts, including 18–27, 20–30, 18–30 and 18–32 years. citeturn0search49

SSC Educational Qualification

Educational qualification is another place where copied summary tables can create confusion. The safe approach is to identify the examination first and then check the qualification written for the post you want.

For CGL, a bachelor's degree is the general educational route for the examination, but some posts have additional requirements. For CHSL, 12th or an equivalent qualification is the basic route for relevant posts, while certain posts can include additional subject requirements. The exact wording in the current notification should be used when your eligibility depends on a particular post.

That matters for questions such as “I have B.Com, can I apply?” or “I studied Arts in 12th, which CHSL posts can I choose?” The answer can depend on the specific post, not just the exam name.

Useful rule: treat a calculator or summary page as a first check. Treat the official SSC notification as the final source for eligibility.

SSC Marks Kaise Calculate Kare?

Marks calculation is easier when you separate the inputs. Start with the number of correct and wrong answers and then apply the marking scheme that belongs to that particular exam or paper.

In a simple negative-marking example, the calculation is:

Raw score = marks from correct answers − applicable negative marks for wrong answers

Unattempted questions normally contribute zero unless the specific marking scheme says otherwise. The important part is not to copy one exam's negative-marking rule into another exam.

Example

Suppose a mock or paper uses 2 marks for each correct answer and 0.25 mark negative marking for each wrong answer. If you have 70 correct and 10 wrong:

70 × 2 − 10 × 0.25 = 140 − 2.5 = 137.5

That is a worked example of the formula, not a universal marking rule for every SSC examination. Before calculating an actual exam score, confirm the applicable marking scheme in the relevant notice.

Raw Marks vs Normalized Marks

This is one of the areas where candidates often get conflicting answers online. Community discussions show questions about whether a cutoff is based on raw or normalized marks and how a candidate's score can change after normalization. citeturn0reddit38turn0reddit44

What are raw marks?

Raw marks are the score obtained directly from your answers using the applicable marking scheme. If you know the correct answers, wrong answers and the marks/negative marks for the paper, you can calculate the raw score.

What are normalized marks?

Where SSC conducts an examination across shifts and applies normalization, the Commission's notified methodology is used to make scores from different shifts comparable. You should not assume that your raw score is automatically your final normalized score.

This distinction matters because candidates sometimes compare a raw mock score with a historical normalized cutoff as if they were the same measurement. They are not necessarily directly comparable.

SSC Cutoff Kya Hota Hai?

“Cutoff” can mean different things in SSC discussions, so first identify which stage and list you are talking about. A qualifying or shortlisting cutoff is not automatically the same thing as the final marks needed for a particular post.

Community discussions also show why candidates can become confused about whether a number is a qualifying cutoff, a post-wise final cutoff or a score being estimated by candidates. citeturn0reddit37turn0reddit41

How should you use previous cutoff data?

Previous cutoff data is useful as historical context. It can help you understand the range of marks seen in a previous recruitment, but it cannot guarantee the cutoff for the next recruitment.

When comparing a previous cutoff, keep the same exam stage, category, post or shortlisting context and scoring basis in view. Also check whether the published figure relates to raw marks or a score after normalization.

Do not treat an online “safe score” as an official number. Social-media estimates are opinions based on incomplete information. The official result or cutoff notice is the source to use for the actual recruitment.
