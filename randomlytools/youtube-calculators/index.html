RPM vs CPM: What Is the Difference?

RPM (revenue per mille) is a creator-focused metric that describes revenue earned per 1,000 views after the relevant revenue share. CPM (cost per mille) is an advertising metric describing the cost advertisers pay for 1,000 ad impressions. They are not interchangeable, so an RPM-based earnings estimate should not be calculated by simply treating CPM as creator income.

How the Earnings Formula Works

A simple planning formula is estimated revenue = views ÷ 1,000 × RPM. For example, if a planning scenario uses 200,000 views and a $4 RPM, the calculation is 200 × $4 = $800. This is only a mathematical example; it is not a claim about a typical or guaranteed RPM.

For a more useful estimate, use a recent RPM from your own YouTube Studio data when you have it. Your actual result can change with audience location, content and format, monetized views, advertiser demand, seasonality, and other channel-specific factors.

Long-Form Videos and Shorts

Do not assume that a long-form RPM can be applied directly to Shorts. Shorts have a different monetization and revenue-sharing system, so their results should be planned separately. If your channel publishes both formats, make separate scenarios instead of combining all views into one RPM calculation.

How to Use These Calculators More Carefully

  1. Start with real data: If your channel is already monetized, use recent YouTube Studio revenue and view data where possible.
  2. Test more than one scenario: Compare a lower, middle and higher RPM assumption instead of relying on one number.
  3. Keep formats separate: Model Shorts and long-form videos independently.
  4. Update your assumptions: Recalculate when your audience, content mix or recent RPM changes.
  5. Treat the result as an estimate: Calculator output cannot guarantee future YouTube revenue.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 views?

There is no single fixed amount for every channel. A planning estimate depends on the RPM you enter, so your own recent YouTube Studio data is more useful than a generic rate.

How much can 1 million YouTube views make?

Use the formula 1,000,000 ÷ 1,000 × RPM. For example, at a hypothetical $5 RPM, the mathematical estimate is $5,000. The example is for understanding the formula, not a typical earnings claim.

Is RPM the same as CPM?

No. RPM and CPM describe different metrics. RPM is useful for creator-side revenue planning, while CPM is an advertising pricing metric.

Are Shorts earnings calculated the same as long-form earnings?

No. Shorts use a different monetization and revenue-sharing model. Use the dedicated Shorts calculator rather than applying a long-form RPM directly to Shorts views.

Can I use these calculators before my channel is monetized?

Yes, you can use them for hypothetical planning. Before monetization, however, the result is only a scenario based on the assumptions you enter and should not be treated as expected income.

Why can actual YouTube revenue differ from a calculator estimate?

Calculator results depend on the inputs you provide. Actual revenue can vary because channel and audience conditions change, and not every view produces the same monetization outcome.
