What Is Taxable Income?

Taxable income is generally the amount of income that remains subject to federal income tax after applicable adjustments and deductions. It is not the same as gross income or adjusted gross income (AGI). Most income is taxable unless a specific tax rule makes it exempt.

This page is a third-party calculator, not an official IRS calculator. The 2026 standard deduction amounts used here are based on IRS-published tax information.

How to Calculate Taxable Income: 5 Steps

For a simplified estimate, first calculate your adjusted gross income (AGI), then subtract the deduction that applies to your tax return. In basic form:

AGI = Total Income − Adjustments to Income

Taxable Income = AGI − Standard or Itemized Deduction − Other Applicable Deductions

For example, if annual income is $75,000, adjustments are $0, and a single filer uses the $16,100 basic 2026 standard deduction, estimated taxable income is $58,900 before any other applicable deductions.

  1. Add taxable income: Include applicable wages, salary, tips, interest, dividends, business income, capital gains, retirement income and other taxable income.
  2. Subtract eligible adjustments: Reduce total income by applicable above-the-line adjustments to estimate AGI.
  3. Choose your deduction: Use the standard deduction or eligible itemized deductions.
  4. Subtract other applicable deductions: Apply deductions that belong in the taxable-income calculation and that you are eligible to claim.
  5. Get estimated taxable income: The remaining amount is the simplified taxable-income estimate used by this calculator.

Quick formula: Gross income − adjustments = AGI; AGI − applicable deductions = estimated taxable income.

How to Calculate Taxable Income From Salary

If your main income is salary, start with your annual taxable income sources and enter the amount in the calculator. Subtract eligible adjustments to estimate AGI, then subtract the standard deduction or eligible itemized deductions. The result is your estimated taxable income.

This is useful when you want to estimate taxable income from salary before looking at federal tax brackets, credits, withholding, or other parts of your tax return.

Taxable Income vs AGI

AGI (adjusted gross income) comes before the standard or itemized deduction. The IRS describes AGI as total income minus certain adjustments. Taxable income is calculated later after applicable deductions are subtracted. This distinction matters because AGI and taxable income are used for different parts of the federal tax calculation.

Standard vs Itemized Deductions

Taxpayers generally choose between the standard deduction and itemizing eligible deductions, subject to the rules that apply to their return. If you choose itemized deductions above, enter the amount you estimate you can actually claim.

As a simple comparison, a single filer with $80,000 of AGI would have $63,900 of taxable income using the $16,100 basic standard deduction, before any other applicable deductions. If eligible itemized deductions total more than the standard deduction, the taxable-income calculation may be lower when itemizing.

What Counts as Taxable Income?

For U.S. federal tax purposes, taxable income can come from multiple sources. Common examples include wages and salary, tips, self-employment or business income, interest, dividends, capital gains, rental income, retirement income, and certain benefits. The IRS explains that AGI starts with taxable income from all sources before applicable adjustments are subtracted.

What Income Is Not Taxable?

Not every payment is necessarily taxable. Some income may be excluded or partially excluded under specific federal rules. Examples can include certain qualified gifts or inheritances, some tax-exempt interest, and other specifically excluded amounts. Do not automatically treat an entire payment as taxable or nontaxable without checking the rule that applies to it.

Taxable Income and Federal Tax Brackets

Your taxable income is used to determine where you fall within the federal income tax rate schedules. For 2026, federal individual rates range from 10% to 37%, but the marginal rate applies only to the portion of taxable income within the applicable bracket. Your final tax can also be affected by credits and other rules.

How Much Tax Do I Pay on My Taxable Income?

Taxable income is one of the main amounts used to determine federal income tax. Your final tax bill can also depend on tax brackets, credits, withholding, additional taxes, and other provisions, so taxable income alone does not determine the amount you owe.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is taxable income?

Taxable income is generally the amount remaining after applicable adjustments and deductions are taken into account.

How do I calculate taxable income?

Start with income, subtract applicable adjustments to estimate AGI, then subtract the standard or itemized deduction and other applicable deductions. The calculator above performs this simplified calculation.

Is taxable income the same as gross income?

No. Gross income is a starting point, while taxable income is generally lower after applicable adjustments and deductions.

What is the 2026 standard deduction?

The basic 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 for single and married filing separately, $24,150 for head of household, and $32,200 for married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse.

Can I use this calculator for salary income?

Yes. Enter your annual salary or other applicable income and any eligible adjustments and deductions to estimate taxable income.

Does this calculate my total federal tax?

No. It estimates taxable income. A complete federal tax calculation can include tax brackets, credits, additional taxes, withholding, and other rules.

Can I use this as an IRS taxable income calculator?

You can use it as a free estimate, but it is not an official IRS calculator. Always check the IRS rules for deductions and special situations that may affect your return.

Taxable Income vs Tax Owed

Your taxable income is not the same thing as the tax you owe. Federal income tax is generally calculated using tax rates and brackets applied to taxable income, while credits, additional taxes, withholding, and other provisions can change the final amount due or refund.
