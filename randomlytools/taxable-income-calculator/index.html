What Is Taxable Income?

Taxable income is the amount of income that remains subject to federal income tax after applicable adjustments and deductions. It is not necessarily the same as your gross income or adjusted gross income (AGI).

For a simplified individual calculation, you can start with income, subtract eligible adjustments to reach AGI, then subtract the applicable standard or itemized deduction and other eligible deductions. The IRS describes AGI as income minus certain adjustments, and taxable income is calculated after applicable deductions.

Taxable Income Formula

A simplified formula is:

Taxable income = AGI − standard or itemized deduction − other applicable deductions

Your actual return may include additional deductions, limitations, credits and special rules. The calculator above is designed as an estimate rather than a substitute for Form 1040 or professional tax preparation.

How to Calculate Taxable Income From Salary

If most of your income comes from salary, start with your applicable income for the year and subtract eligible adjustments to determine AGI. Then subtract the standard deduction or eligible itemized deductions and any other deductions that apply. The result is your estimated taxable income.

Taxable Income vs AGI

AGI is calculated before the standard or itemized deduction. Taxable income comes later in the calculation after deductions are applied. The IRS describes AGI as total income minus certain adjustments, while Form 1040 calculates taxable income after deductions.

Standard vs Itemized Deductions

In general, taxpayers may use the standard deduction or itemize eligible deductions. If you choose itemized deductions in this calculator, enter the estimated total of deductions you are eligible to claim. The IRS notes that taxpayers generally choose between the standard deduction and itemizing, subject to specific eligibility rules.

What Counts as Taxable Income?

Taxable income can come from many sources, including wages, self-employment, business income, interest, dividends, capital gains, retirement distributions and certain benefits. Whether a specific payment is taxable depends on the applicable tax rules.

How Much Tax Do I Pay on My Taxable Income?

Taxable income is the base used to determine federal income tax under the applicable tax-rate schedules. Your final tax bill can also be affected by credits, additional taxes, withholding and other provisions, so taxable income alone does not tell you your final amount due.

2026 Tax Changes to Know

For 2026, the IRS lists a number of updated deductions and tax provisions. For example, the standard deduction amounts increased, and certain new or enhanced deductions may apply to qualified seniors, tipped workers, overtime and vehicle-loan interest subject to eligibility and phase-out rules.

Frequently Asked Questions About Taxable Income

What is taxable income?

Taxable income is the amount remaining after applicable adjustments and deductions are taken into account.

How do I calculate taxable income?

Start with income, subtract applicable adjustments to calculate AGI, then subtract the standard or itemized deduction and other applicable deductions.

Is taxable income the same as gross income?

No. Gross income is a starting point. Taxable income is generally lower after applicable adjustments and deductions.

What is the 2026 standard deduction?

The basic 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 for single and married filing separately, $24,150 for head of household, and $32,200 for married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse.

Can I use this calculator for salary income?

Yes. Enter your annual income and applicable adjustments and deductions to estimate taxable income.

Does this calculate my total federal tax?

No. It estimates taxable income. It does not replace a full federal tax calculation with every credit, additional tax and special rule.
