How to Calculate Percentage of Marks

The standard formula for calculating percentage of marks is (Marks Obtained ÷ Total Marks) × 100. For example, if you score 450 marks out of 500, your percentage is 90%.

Marks Percentage Calculator

Use the first mode when you know the marks obtained and maximum marks. The calculator works with different total marks, so it can be used for individual tests, exams and assignments.

When to Use Each Calculator Mode

The three modes answer slightly different questions. Marks Percentage is for a single score, Overall Percentage combines marks from multiple subjects, and Target Marks works backward from a target percentage to show the corresponding target score.

  • Single exam or test: enter obtained marks and maximum marks.
  • Several subjects: add each subject's obtained and maximum marks.
  • Target planning: enter your current marks, total maximum marks, and target percentage.

Grade Percentage Calculator for Multiple Subjects

Use Overall Percentage to enter marks for several subjects. The calculator adds the obtained marks and maximum marks before calculating the combined percentage. This avoids averaging subject percentages when the subjects have different maximum marks.

Target Percentage and Required Marks

The Target Marks mode calculates how many total marks correspond to your target percentage and shows the additional marks required from your current score. If the current score already reaches the target, the result shows that no additional marks are required.

Percentage Formula for Marks

Percentage = (Obtained Marks ÷ Maximum Marks) × 100

To calculate the marks corresponding to a percentage: Required Marks = Maximum Marks × Target Percentage ÷ 100.

Why Overall Percentage Should Use Total Marks

When subjects have different maximum marks, calculating each subject percentage and then taking a simple average can give a different result from the combined percentage. This calculator combines the obtained marks and maximum marks first, then applies the standard percentage formula.

For example, if one subject is out of 100 and another is out of 50, their maximum marks are not equal. Adding both obtained scores and dividing by the combined maximum preserves the actual weighting created by the exam marks.

Worked Percentage Examples

  • 450 out of 500: (450 ÷ 500) × 100 = 90%
  • 72 out of 100: (72 ÷ 100) × 100 = 72%
  • 38 out of 50: (38 ÷ 50) × 100 = 76%
  • 270 out of 300: (270 ÷ 300) × 100 = 90%

Example for different subject totals: if you score 45/50 and 70/100, the combined percentage is (45 + 70) ÷ (50 + 100) × 100 = 76.67%. Simply averaging 90% and 70% would give 80%, which is not the combined marks percentage because the subject totals are different.

Important Notes Before Using the Result

A percentage is a mathematical calculation from the marks you enter. It does not by itself determine a school's grade, rank, pass/fail status, admission eligibility, or cutoff. Those rules can vary by exam, institution, board, or recruitment process.

For official applications, compare your calculated percentage with the exact calculation method stated in the relevant notification or marksheet instructions. If an authority requires a particular rounding method, use that method rather than assuming that every system rounds percentages in the same way.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate percentage of marks?

Divide marks obtained by total marks and multiply by 100.

How do I calculate marks percentage?

Enter your obtained marks and maximum marks in the calculator above.

What is a grade percentage calculator?

It calculates the percentage represented by marks and can also combine marks across multiple subjects.

How do I calculate overall percentage for multiple subjects?

Add all obtained marks, add all maximum marks, then calculate total obtained divided by total maximum multiplied by 100.

How many marks do I need for a target percentage?

Multiply the maximum marks by the target percentage divided by 100, then compare that target mark with your current score.

Should I average subject percentages to calculate overall percentage?

Not when subjects have different maximum marks. Combine obtained marks and maximum marks first, then calculate the overall percentage.

Can this calculator calculate a target percentage for 500 marks?

Yes. For example, 90% of 500 marks is 450 marks. Use Target Marks mode with 500 maximum marks and a 90% target.

Does percentage determine my grade or admission eligibility?

No. Percentage is a mathematical result. Grade, pass or fail, admission eligibility and cutoff rules depend on the relevant institution or official notification.
