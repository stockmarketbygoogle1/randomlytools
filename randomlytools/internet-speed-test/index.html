How This Internet Speed Test Works

This browser-based test measures three practical connection metrics: download bandwidth, upload bandwidth, and latency. When you start the test, the page performs a sequence of network measurements and converts the measured bandwidth to Mbps and latency to milliseconds. The result is a measurement of your connection under the conditions that existed when you ran the test.

The test is useful for checking your connection, but it is not an ISP certification or a guarantee of the maximum speed available on your broadband plan. Server conditions, routing, Wi-Fi, device load and other network activity can affect the result. For a troubleshooting decision, repeat the test and compare results rather than treating one measurement as definitive.

What Is an Internet Speed Test?

An internet speed test measures how quickly data can move between your device and a test server. The main measurements are download speed, upload speed and latency. Download and upload bandwidth are normally shown in megabits per second (Mbps), while latency is shown in milliseconds (ms).

How to Check Internet Speed

  1. Connect your device to the internet you want to test.
  2. Close large downloads, uploads and bandwidth-heavy apps where practical.
  3. Click Start Speed Test.
  4. Wait for the measurements to complete.
  5. Review your download speed, upload speed and latency.

Download Speed vs Upload Speed

Download speed describes data transferred from the test server to your device. It matters for activities such as loading websites, streaming video and downloading files.

Upload speed describes data transferred from your device to the test server. It matters for activities such as sending large files, video calls, live streaming and cloud backups.

What Is Ping or Latency?

Latency is the time taken for a network request to travel and receive a response. A lower latency usually means a quicker response from the network. Latency can be especially noticeable in interactive activities such as online gaming and video calls.

Why Is My Internet Speed Different From My Plan?

A speed-test result can differ from the advertised or subscribed speed because of Wi-Fi signal quality, router performance, network congestion, device limitations, background traffic, server distance and other conditions. A single test is a measurement at one point in time, not a permanent specification of your connection.

Wi-Fi Speed Test Tips

  • Test from the same location where you normally use the connection.
  • For comparison, repeat the test at different times of day.
  • Compare Wi-Fi with a wired Ethernet connection when available.
  • Keep other high-bandwidth devices as idle as practical during the test.
  • Restarting network equipment can change conditions, but it should not be necessary before every measurement.

Internet Speed Test vs Broadband Speed Test

These terms generally describe the same basic type of measurement: checking the performance of an internet connection. The connection may be fiber, cable, DSL, fixed wireless, mobile broadband or another access technology.

What Is a Good Internet Speed?

A “good” internet speed depends on how the connection is used. Browsing, messaging and light everyday use can work with less bandwidth than a household that streams high-resolution video, uploads large files, joins video meetings and connects many devices at the same time. Your speed-test result should therefore be compared with your actual needs and the plan you pay for, rather than with one universal number.

  • Web browsing and social media: consistency and low latency can matter as much as raw download speed.
  • HD or 4K streaming: higher download bandwidth helps when several people or devices stream at once.
  • Video calls: stable download and upload performance plus low latency are important for a responsive call.
  • Online gaming: latency, jitter, packet loss and connection stability can be important alongside download speed.
  • Large uploads and cloud backups: upload speed becomes especially important.

Understanding Speed-Test Results

Use the three results together. Download speed tells you about data received by your device, upload speed tells you about data sent from your device, and latency indicates responsiveness. A high download number does not automatically mean every application will feel fast: gaming, calls and other interactive tasks can also be affected by latency, jitter or packet loss, which this page does not currently report.

If your result is much lower than expected, repeat the test under controlled conditions and compare Wi-Fi with Ethernet when possible. Mbps vs MB/s: What Is the Difference?

Internet providers and speed-test tools normally report bandwidth in Mbps, meaning megabits per second. File downloads may be displayed in MB/s, meaning megabytes per second. Because one byte contains eight bits, the two units are not the same. For example, 100 Mbps is theoretically about 12.5 MB/s before normal protocol and network overhead.

Why Is My Internet Slow Even With a Fast Plan?

A high-speed broadband or fiber plan does not guarantee the same result on every device and at every moment. Your actual test can be affected by the Wi-Fi band, distance from the router, walls, interference, router capacity, device hardware, background applications, VPN use, browser activity, local network traffic and congestion outside your home.

If download speed is consistently much lower than expected, run several tests under similar conditions. If possible, compare a wired Ethernet test with Wi-Fi. If Ethernet is substantially faster, the local Wi-Fi connection may be the limiting factor rather than the broadband line itself.

How to Get a More Accurate Speed-Test Result

  1. Use the device and connection you actually want to evaluate.
  2. Pause large downloads, cloud backups, video uploads and other heavy traffic.
  3. Close unnecessary tabs and bandwidth-heavy applications.
  4. For Wi-Fi testing, stay in your normal usage location and keep the device reasonably close to the router when comparing results.
  5. Run more than one test and compare results instead of relying on a single measurement.
  6. Test at different times if you suspect evening or peak-hour congestion.
  7. When available, compare Wi-Fi with Ethernet to separate Wi-Fi limitations from the internet connection itself.

Fiber, Broadband, Wi-Fi, 4G and 5G Speed Tests

You can use an internet speed test for many connection types, including fiber broadband, cable, DSL, fixed wireless, home Wi-Fi and mobile data. On 4G or 5G, results can vary with signal strength, network load, location and the mobile network serving the device. On home broadband, the result can also vary between a wired connection and Wi-Fi.

If you are checking a Jio, Airtel, Vi, BSNL, fiber or other connection, the useful approach is to test under consistent conditions and compare the result with the plan or service information provided by your provider. A speed test measures your connection at that moment; it does not by itself diagnose every cause of slow internet.

What to Do If Your Internet Speed Is Too Low

First, repeat the test to make sure the result is not a temporary fluctuation. Then check whether the issue occurs on one device or multiple devices. If only one device is slow, its Wi-Fi signal, software, browser, network adapter or background activity may be relevant. If several devices are slow, compare Wi-Fi with Ethernet if possible and check the router and broadband connection.

If consistently low results remain after repeated tests, record the approximate time, connection type and measurements. That information can make it easier to discuss the issue with your internet service provider.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check my internet speed?

Click Start Speed Test above and wait for the download, upload and latency measurements to finish.

What is a good internet speed?

There is no single speed that is ideal for everyone. The amount of bandwidth you need depends on the number of users, devices and activities on the connection.

What does Mbps mean?

Mbps means megabits per second. It is a common unit used to describe internet download and upload bandwidth.

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Mbps means megabits per second, while MB/s means megabytes per second. Since one byte is eight bits, the numerical values are different.

Why does my Wi-Fi speed change?

Wi-Fi performance can change because of signal strength, interference, distance from the router, network congestion and other devices using the connection.

Why are speed-test results different each time?

Internet conditions change over time. Background traffic, congestion, Wi-Fi conditions, device load and test-server conditions can all affect a measurement.

Why is my download speed good but upload speed low?

Download and upload capacity can differ by connection plan and network conditions. Wi-Fi, device activity, network congestion and provider-side limits can also affect upload performance.

Can I test my 5G or mobile internet speed?

Yes. You can run the test on a mobile data connection. For useful comparisons, keep the location, signal conditions and device setup as consistent as possible.

Does a speed test measure my Wi-Fi speed or internet speed?

When you run the test over Wi-Fi, the result reflects the performance between your device, Wi-Fi network and the internet test server. A wired Ethernet test can help separate Wi-Fi limitations from the wider internet connection.

How many times should I run an internet speed test?

Run several tests when you are troubleshooting. Comparing multiple results at similar and different times can reveal whether the performance is stable or changing.
