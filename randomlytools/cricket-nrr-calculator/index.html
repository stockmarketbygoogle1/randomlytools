What Is Net Run Rate (NRR) in Cricket?

Net Run Rate, usually called NRR, compares how quickly a team scores with how quickly it concedes runs. It is commonly used as a tie-breaker in limited-overs competitions when teams finish level on points and the tournament regulations specify NRR.

A positive NRR means the team's scoring rate is higher than its rate of conceding runs. A negative NRR means the opposite. The number itself is expressed as runs per over.

Cricket NRR Formula

For a basic completed-innings calculation, use:

NRR = (Runs Scored ÷ Overs Faced) − (Runs Conceded ÷ Overs Bowled)

Example: a team scores 160 in 20 overs and concedes 150 in 20 overs. Its scoring rate is 160 ÷ 20 = 8.000, while the opposition's rate is 150 ÷ 20 = 7.500. Therefore, NRR = 8.000 − 7.500 = +0.500.

Worked Example With Cricket Over Notation

Suppose a team scores 145 runs in 19.3 overs and concedes 150 runs in 20 overs. In cricket notation, 19.3 means 19 overs and 3 legal balls. Since six legal balls make an over, that is 117 legal balls, or 19.5 mathematical overs.

The scoring rate is therefore 145 ÷ 19.5 = about 7.436, and the opposition rate is 150 ÷ 20 = 7.500. The basic NRR is about −0.064. This illustrates why treating 19.3 as ordinary decimal 19.3 would give the wrong result.

How to Enter Cricket Overs Correctly

Cricket over notation is written as completed overs.balls. The digit after the decimal point represents legal balls, so it can only be 0 through 5.

  • 20.0 = 20 overs = 120 legal balls
  • 19.3 = 19 overs and 3 balls = 117 legal balls
  • 10.5 = 10 overs and 5 balls = 65 legal balls
  • 10.6 is not valid cricket over notation

This calculator converts the notation to the mathematical number of overs before calculating the run rates.

NRR for IPL, T20, ODI and Other Tournaments

The basic formula is useful for understanding NRR in IPL, T20, ODI and other limited-overs contexts, but the official calculation is governed by the relevant competition's playing conditions. Different situations can require special treatment, including an innings ending early, an abandoned match, or a result affected by a rain-adjustment method.

For that reason, use this tool as a calculation and scenario aid rather than as a replacement for the official tournament regulations.

NRR and Qualification Scenarios

NRR by itself does not tell you whether a team will qualify. A qualification scenario can depend on current points, remaining fixtures, results involving other teams, and the tie-break rules used by the competition.

All-Out Innings and Official NRR

When a team is dismissed before using its full overs, the official competition rules may specify how that innings contributes to NRR. The direct formula shown above is useful for understanding the calculation, but the number produced by this tool should not automatically be treated as the official points-table figure in every competition.

Important Notes Before Using the Result

  • Enter runs as whole numbers unless the competition's data source gives another format.
  • Enter overs in cricket notation, not ordinary decimal notation.
  • Check that the overs correspond to the correct innings and team.
  • A positive or negative NRR is not itself a qualification guarantee.
  • For official standings, follow the tournament's current published playing conditions and tie-break rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NRR in cricket?

NRR is the difference between a team's run-scoring rate and the opposition's run-scoring rate, calculated according to the relevant competition rules.

What is the NRR formula?

For a basic completed-innings calculation, divide runs scored by overs faced, divide runs conceded by overs bowled, then subtract the second rate from the first.

How do I enter 19.3 overs?

Enter 19.3. It means 19 complete overs plus 3 legal balls, which is 117 legal balls or 19.5 mathematical overs.

Can NRR be negative?

Yes. NRR is negative when the team's run-scoring rate is lower than the opposition's run-scoring rate.

Does an all-out innings affect official NRR?

It can. Competition rules may specify special treatment for an innings that ends before the full over allocation is used, so check the official playing conditions.

Does NRR always decide qualification?

No. The tournament regulations decide which tie-breaker applies and when NRR is used.

Can this calculator predict IPL qualification?

It can help calculate NRR scenarios, but qualification also depends on points, remaining results and the competition's tie-break rules.
