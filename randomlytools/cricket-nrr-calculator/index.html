How to Calculate Net Run Rate in Cricket

For a basic completed-innings calculation, NRR is the difference between a team's runs-per-over rate and its opponents' runs-per-over rate.

NRR = (Runs Scored ÷ Overs Faced) − (Runs Conceded ÷ Overs Bowled)

How Cricket Overs Are Converted

Cricket overs are not decimal numbers. For example, 19.3 means 19 overs and 3 legal balls. Since one over contains 6 legal balls, 19.3 corresponds to 117 balls. This distinction is important for accurate NRR calculations.

NRR and All-Out Innings

Official competition regulations can specify how overs are counted when a team is dismissed before using its full allocation. The calculator gives the direct mathematical rate from the inputs you enter; use the tournament's official playing conditions for a live qualification decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NRR in cricket?

Net Run Rate measures the difference between a team's scoring rate and its opponents' scoring rate, usually expressed as runs per over.

What does 19.3 overs mean?

It means 19 complete overs plus 3 legal balls, not 19.3 decimal overs.

Does NRR always decide qualification?

No. The official tournament regulations determine the tie-break order.
