What Is Net Run Rate (NRR) in Cricket?

Net Run Rate, commonly called NRR, measures how quickly a cricket team scores runs compared with how quickly it concedes runs. It is widely used in limited-overs league tables when tournament rules require a tie-break between teams on the same points.

Use this free Cricket NRR Calculator to calculate a basic NRR from completed innings. It is useful for IPL, T20, ODI and other competitions where the relevant playing conditions use Net Run Rate.

Cricket NRR Formula

For a basic completed-innings calculation, the formula is:

NRR = (Runs Scored ÷ Overs Faced) − (Runs Conceded ÷ Overs Bowled)

For example, 160 runs from 20 overs gives a scoring rate of 8.000. If the opposition scores 150 from 20 overs, its rate is 7.500. Therefore, NRR = 8.000 − 7.500 = +0.500.

How to Enter Cricket Overs Correctly

Cricket overs are written in overs-and-balls notation, not normal decimal notation. 19.3 means 19 completed overs plus 3 legal balls. Because one over contains 6 legal balls, 19.3 is 117 legal balls, or 19.5 overs mathematically.

This calculator converts the cricket notation before calculating the rate, so entering 19.3 does not incorrectly treat it as 19.3 decimal overs.

NRR Calculator for IPL and Other Tournaments

The same basic mathematical NRR formula is useful when checking an IPL or other limited-overs points table, but official tournament regulations matter for qualification. All-out innings, abandoned matches, DLS-adjusted results and competition-specific tie-break rules can affect the official calculation.

For a qualification question, first identify the possible points-table outcomes and then use the NRR calculation relevant to the scenario.

NRR and Qualification Scenarios

When several teams finish on equal points, NRR can become the difference between qualifying and elimination if the tournament rules use NRR at that stage. A useful scenario analysis therefore combines points, remaining fixtures and the NRR threshold that must be beaten.

NRR and All-Out Innings

Official playing conditions can specify how overs are counted when a team is dismissed before using its full allocation. The calculator above performs the direct calculation from the overs you enter; for an official tournament qualification decision, follow that competition's published rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NRR in cricket?

NRR is the difference between a team's run-scoring rate and its opponents' run-scoring rate, calculated according to the relevant competition rules.

How do I calculate NRR?

For a basic completed-innings calculation, divide runs scored by overs faced, divide runs conceded by overs bowled, and subtract the second rate from the first.

What does 19.3 overs mean?

It means 19 complete overs plus 3 legal balls. It is 117 legal balls, not 19.3 decimal overs.

Does NRR always decide qualification?

No. The official tournament rules decide which tie-breaker is used and when NRR applies.
