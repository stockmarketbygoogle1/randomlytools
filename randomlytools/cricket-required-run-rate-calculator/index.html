How to Calculate Required Run Rate in Cricket

Required Run Rate (RRR) tells a chasing team how many runs it must score per over to reach the target in the overs remaining.

Required Run Rate = Runs Required ÷ Overs Remaining

For example, if a team needs 100 runs from 10 overs, it needs 10.00 runs per over.

Why Cricket Overs Are Different From Decimal Overs

In a cricket scorecard, 10.3 means 10 overs and 3 balls. It does not mean 10.3 decimal overs. Since an over contains 6 legal balls, 10.3 corresponds to 10.5 mathematical overs.

Required Run Rate in a Qualification Scenario

When a team needs a particular NRR to qualify, the required chase speed can depend on the target NRR, existing tournament totals and the exact playing conditions. Frequently Asked Questions

What is required run rate?

It is the average number of runs per over a team needs from the remaining overs to reach the target.

How is RRR calculated?

Subtract the current score from the target, then divide the runs required by the mathematical number of overs remaining.

What does 10.3 overs mean?

It means 10 overs and 3 legal balls, equal to 10.5 overs mathematically.
