What Is Required Run Rate in Cricket?

Required Run Rate (RRR) is the scoring rate a chasing team needs from the remaining overs to reach its target. It is usually shown as runs per over.

Required Run Rate = Runs Required ÷ Mathematical Overs Remaining

For example, if a team needs 100 runs from 10 overs, it needs 10.00 runs per over.

How to Calculate RRR

First subtract the current score from the target. Then convert the remaining cricket overs into legal balls and calculate the required scoring rate. This avoids treating cricket notation such as 10.3 as 10.3 decimal overs.

Cricket Overs to Balls

In cricket, 10.3 means 10 completed overs plus 3 legal balls: 63 balls in total, or 10.5 mathematical overs. Similarly, 19.3 means 117 balls. The number after the decimal is balls, not a decimal fraction of an over.

RRR and Cricket Chase Scenarios

A high required run rate means the chasing team must score quickly. The RRR can change after every over and every scoring event. For qualification situations where NRR matters, the exact chase completion time can also affect a team's final Net Run Rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is required run rate in cricket?

It is the average number of runs per over a team needs from the remaining overs to reach its target.

How is required run rate calculated?

Runs required are calculated as target minus current score. Divide that number by the mathematical overs remaining to get runs per over.

What does 10.3 overs mean?

It means 10 overs and 3 legal balls, equal to 63 balls or 10.5 mathematical overs.

What is the difference between run rate and required run rate?

Run rate describes the scoring rate already achieved, while required run rate describes the scoring rate needed from the remaining overs.
