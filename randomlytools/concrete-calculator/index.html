How Much Concrete Do I Need?

This concrete calculator estimates the volume needed for common rectangular and cylindrical projects. Enter the dimensions of your slab, footing, wall, column or post hole, then choose a waste allowance. The result shows the base volume and the amount after adding the selected allowance.

For most small projects, the useful shopping number is not only the exact mathematical volume. You may also need extra material for uneven ground, measurement differences, spillage or other practical losses. A waste allowance lets you see that adjusted quantity without changing the underlying dimensions.

How to Use the Concrete Calculator

  1. Choose the project type: Select slab/patio, footing, wall, column or post hole.
  2. Enter the dimensions: Use the fields shown for the selected shape. For feet, enter thickness in feet (4 inches is about 0.3333 feet).
  3. Choose units: Use feet, meters or centimeters.
  4. Select waste: Add 5%, 10% or another available allowance when planning an order.
  5. Calculate: Review cubic feet, cubic yards, cubic meters and approximate bag quantities.

How to Calculate Concrete

For a rectangular slab, the basic volume formula is:

Volume = Length × Width × Thickness

When all three measurements are in feet, the result is cubic feet. To convert cubic feet to cubic yards:

Cubic yards = Cubic feet ÷ 27

To convert cubic feet to cubic meters, multiply by approximately 0.0283168. Keeping units consistent is important: if thickness is entered in inches while length and width are in feet, convert the thickness to feet first.

Concrete Calculator for Slabs and Patios

For a rectangular slab, patio, walkway or pad, enter length, width and thickness. For example, a 10 ft × 10 ft slab that is 4 inches thick (0.3333 ft) has a base volume of about 33.33 cubic feet, or 1.23 cubic yards, before adding waste.

Slab thickness depends on the project and site requirements. This calculator only estimates volume; it does not determine the structural thickness, reinforcement or base preparation required for a particular construction project.

Concrete Calculator for Footings

Footings can be estimated when their shape and dimensions are known. For a rectangular footing, use length, width and depth. If your footing is part of a structural design, use the dimensions specified by the applicable plan or qualified professional rather than choosing a size from a calculator.

Concrete Calculator for Walls

For a straight rectangular wall, the volume can be estimated from wall length, height and thickness. Enter those three dimensions in the wall mode. For walls with openings, irregular sections or multiple segments, calculate each section separately and combine the results.

Concrete Calculator for Columns and Post Holes

Round columns and post holes use a cylinder formula:

Volume = π × radius² × height

If you know the diameter instead of the radius, divide the diameter by two before applying the formula. The calculator accepts diameter and depth for these cylindrical project types.

How Many Bags of Concrete Do I Need?

Bag estimates are useful when you are mixing packaged concrete rather than ordering ready-mix. The number of bags depends on the yield printed on the specific product, so there is no single universal number of bags per cubic yard.

This calculator gives approximate 40 lb, 60 lb, 80 lb and 25 kg bag counts using common planning yields. Always check the manufacturer's bag yield and follow the product instructions before purchasing or mixing.

Concrete in Cubic Feet, Cubic Yards and Cubic Meters

How Much Concrete Should I Order?

Start with the calculated volume, then consider a reasonable allowance for waste and the practical ordering rules of your supplier. A mathematical result such as 1.23 cubic yards does not necessarily mean a supplier will sell or deliver exactly 1.23 cubic yards. Ready-mix companies can have minimum-load, short-load, delivery-area and scheduling requirements.

For a ready-mix order, use the calculator as an estimate and confirm the final order quantity with the supplier. For bagged concrete, use the actual yield printed on the bag.

Concrete Cost Calculator: What You Need to Know

Concrete cost depends on location, supplier, mix, delivery, quantity, pumping, labor and other project conditions. Because prices vary, this page focuses on quantity rather than presenting a misleading universal concrete price. If you know your supplier's price per cubic yard or per bag, multiply that price by the quantity you plan to purchase.

Common Concrete Calculation Mistakes

  • Mixing units: Do not multiply feet by inches without converting them to compatible units.
  • Forgetting thickness: A slab's area alone does not give its volume.
  • Ignoring waste: The mathematical minimum may not be the practical amount to order.
  • Assuming all bags have the same yield: Check the product label.
  • Using the calculator as a structural design: Volume estimation does not replace engineering, building-code or site-specific requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a concrete calculator?

A concrete calculator estimates how much concrete is needed from project dimensions. It can express the result in cubic feet, cubic yards and cubic meters and can add a selected waste allowance.

How do I calculate concrete for a slab?

Multiply the slab length by width by thickness after converting all measurements to the same unit. Divide cubic feet by 27 to convert the result to cubic yards.

How many bags of concrete do I need?

Divide the required volume by the yield of the particular bag. Because bag yields vary, use the yield printed on the product package. This calculator provides approximate bag counts for common bag sizes.

How much concrete do I need for a 10×10 slab?

At 4 inches thick, a 10×10 ft slab needs about 33.33 cubic feet, or 1.23 cubic yards, before waste.

How much concrete do I need for a 12×12 slab?

At 4 inches thick, a 12×12 ft slab needs about 48 cubic feet, or 1.78 cubic yards, before waste.

How much concrete do I need for a 20×20 slab?

At 4 inches thick, a 20×20 ft slab needs about 133.33 cubic feet, or 4.94 cubic yards, before waste.

How much waste should I add?

A common planning approach is to allow some extra material for uneven conditions, spillage and measurement differences. The calculator lets you compare 0%, 5%, 10% and 15% allowances. The appropriate amount depends on the project.

Can I calculate concrete in cubic yards?

Yes. Enter the dimensions and the calculator converts the result to cubic yards. One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet.
