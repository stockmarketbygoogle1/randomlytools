YouTube RPM Calculator 2026

Calculate your YouTube revenue per 1,000 views using your actual revenue and total views. See your RPM instantly and use it to estimate earnings for 1K, 10K, 100K and 1M views.

What Is YouTube RPM?

YouTube RPM means Revenue Per Mille, or revenue earned per 1,000 views. It is a creator-focused metric that helps you understand how much revenue your channel generated relative to total views.

For forecasting, your actual YouTube Studio RPM is usually more useful than a generic RPM benchmark because RPM can vary by audience, niche, monetization, geography and time of year.

YouTube RPM Formula

RPM = Revenue ÷ Total Views × 1,000.

For example, if a channel generated ₹800 from 1,000,000 views, its RPM would be ₹0.80. If the same revenue came from 100,000 views, the RPM would be ₹8.00.

Why Does YouTube RPM Change?

RPM is not a permanent channel setting. It can change with audience country, niche, content format, seasonality, advertiser demand, monetization coverage, viewer behaviour and the mix of traffic your videos receive.

YouTube RPM vs CPM: What Is the Difference?

CPM generally describes what advertisers pay for ad impressions or opportunities, while RPM is a creator-side revenue measure. A high CPM does not automatically mean the same value as RPM because they account for different parts of the monetization process.

What Is a Good YouTube RPM?

There is no single RPM that is good for every channel. Compare your current RPM with your own historical RPM and consider your niche and audience. Country and niche benchmarks can be useful for planning, but they should not replace your Studio data.

Worked YouTube RPM Examples

Suppose a channel earned ₹1,500 from 300,000 total views. Using RPM = revenue ÷ views × 1,000, the RPM is ₹5. If the same ₹1,500 came from 150,000 views, the RPM would be ₹10. The calculator measures the relationship between revenue and total views; it does not predict future revenue by itself.

How to Turn RPM Into an Earnings Estimate

Once you know your RPM, reverse the formula to estimate revenue: estimated revenue = views ÷ 1,000 × RPM. For example, at ₹8 RPM, 50,000 views would be about ₹400 and 500,000 views would be about ₹4,000. These are mathematical scenarios. Actual future revenue can change if your RPM changes.

Use Recent Data for a More Useful RPM

For a monetized channel, calculate RPM using a consistent period such as the last 28 or 90 days rather than mixing unrelated time periods. Keep the revenue and views from the same reporting period and currency. If your channel covers several formats or audience markets, your blended RPM can change as the traffic mix changes.

What Can Make an RPM Estimate Less Reliable?

  • Changing audience geography: the countries represented in your views can change the revenue mix.
  • Seasonality: advertiser demand can vary during different times of the year.
  • Content mix: long-form videos and Shorts use different monetization models.
  • View and revenue periods: combining figures from different periods can produce a misleading RPM.
  • Future performance: a past RPM is useful for planning, but it is not a guarantee of future earnings.

Frequently Asked Questions About YouTube RPM

What is YouTube RPM?

YouTube RPM is creator revenue per 1,000 views. It helps compare revenue performance across different periods and view counts.

How do I calculate YouTube RPM?

Use RPM = revenue ÷ total views × 1,000. Enter the same currency for revenue throughout the calculation.

Is YouTube RPM the same as CPM?

No. RPM is a creator-side revenue metric, while CPM is mainly an advertiser-side metric related to ad impressions.

What is a good YouTube RPM?

There is no universal RPM target for every channel. Compare your RPM with your own historical performance and consider audience, niche, format and seasonality.

How much is 1,000 views worth at ₹5 RPM?

At ₹5 RPM, 1,000 views would correspond to about ₹5 in the same currency.

How much is 1 million views worth at ₹10 RPM?

At ₹10 RPM, 1 million views would correspond to about ₹10,000. This is a mathematical estimate, not a guaranteed payout.

Why is my RPM lower than my CPM?

RPM and CPM measure different things. RPM considers creator revenue relative to total views, while CPM is an advertiser-side metric for ad impressions.

Can RPM change from month to month?

Yes. Audience geography, advertiser demand, monetized views, seasonality, content and other factors can change RPM over time.

Can I use this calculator for Shorts?

You can calculate a mathematical RPM, but Shorts use a different monetization model. Use the dedicated Shorts calculator for Shorts-specific planning.
