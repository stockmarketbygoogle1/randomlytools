How Much Do YouTube Shorts Pay for 1,000 Views?

There is no fixed YouTube Shorts payment for 1,000 views. Shorts use a different revenue-sharing model from long-form videos, and actual earnings depend on eligible engaged views, audience geography, the Creator Pool and other monetization factors. YouTube says monetizing Shorts creators receive 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Creator Pool.

Shorts RPM can be much lower than long-form RPM, so large view counts do not automatically mean large ad revenue. Use your own YouTube Studio data whenever available; this calculator is a planning estimate, not a payout guarantee.

YouTube Shorts Earnings Formula

For a simple RPM-based scenario, earnings = views ÷ 1,000 × Shorts RPM. For example, at a $0.10 RPM, 1 million views gives a mathematical estimate of $100. Your real Shorts RPM can be different.

How YouTube Shorts Monetization Works

Shorts Feed ads are pooled rather than being treated like an ad directly attached to one long-form video. YouTube allocates the Creator Pool based on monetizing creators’ share of eligible engaged Shorts views, then applies the creator revenue share. This is why a long-form RPM should not be used directly to forecast Shorts earnings.

YouTube also notes that eligible Shorts revenue can include YouTube Premium revenue, while ineligible or artificial views are not included in the monetization calculation.

Does Audience Country Affect YouTube Shorts RPM?

Yes. Audience geography can influence the revenue available in the Shorts system. A channel with mostly Indian viewers can have a different effective RPM profile from a channel with a mostly US, UK or other international audience. Country figures in this calculator are planning assumptions, not official YouTube RPM rates.

How Many Shorts Views Do You Need to Make $100 or $1,000?

At a $0.10 RPM, about 1 million views would be needed for $100 and 10 million views for $1,000. At a $0.20 RPM, those requirements would be roughly half.
